Marble Galton Board

Every marble takes a random path through the pegs. No single marble is predictable, but together they always build the same bell curve.

At each peg a marble goes left or right, roughly a coin flip. Add up many coin flips and you get the normal distribution: most marbles end near the middle, few reach the edges. This is the central limit theorem, the same reason heights, test scores and measurement errors form bell curves. Tilt or wind adds a steady push to every flip, so the whole curve slides sideways but keeps its shape.
